Opus 5.5Fable 5.2
Read the full prompt 
The Lost Temple: a complete, playable 3D first-person relic-heist game that runs in the browser.

Premise
You're a treasure hunter who reaches an ancient ruined temple at dawn. Work your way from the sunlit jungle through roofless ruins and down into pitch-black dungeons, grabbing as much valuable loot as you can. Then escape through the great temple door before darkness falls. At sunset, the temple's guardians wake up and hunt you. Greed against the clock is the whole game.

Constraints
- Web app in TypeScript or JavaScript, using Three.js for rendering and a real physics engine (Rapier or cannon-es). No full game engines such as Unity, Godot or PlayCanvas.
- Runs from `npm install && npm run dev`. Include a README with run steps and controls.
- Every asset is generated in code: procedural geometry, canvas or shader textures, and WebAudio sound. No external models, images or audio.
- At least 60 FPS on a mid-range laptop with shadows on.

01 Scene progression
The run is a chain of at least 7 distinct scenes, streamed in and out with seamless transitions and no loading screens in the middle of a run. They're grouped into 3 zones.

Zone A: Outside (full sunlight)
- Jungle approach: dense vegetation, a river crossing, and the first collapsing rope bridge over a gorge.
- Temple courtyard: overgrown plazas, toppled pillars, a sundial puzzle that uses the real sun position, and the great door. The door is your entrance and your only exit.
- Outer cliffs: an optional scene with ledges, vine climbing, wind, and high-value relics out in the open.

Zone B: Roofless ruins (partly open to the sky)
- Broken halls: the roof has caved in, so shafts of sunlight move across the floor through the day. Some paths are only safe while they're sunlit.
- Collapsed sanctum: half the ceiling is gone and the rest is unstable. Noise brings debris down. The main idol sits here under a beam of light.

Zone C: Fully dungeoned (no sky at all)
- Lower catacombs: crumbling floors, dart corridors, and a flooded chamber.
- Deep vault: the richest and heaviest loot, the longest way back out, and the guardians' lair next door.

Progression rules
- Each zone is more dangerous and holds more valuable loot than the one before.
- Deeper scenes unlock through puzzles, keys or relics found earlier (for example, a sun-disc from the courtyard opens the catacomb seal).
- Shortcuts open once you've used them, like a kicked-down wall or a lowered drawbridge. The escape route should be its own design, not just the way in played backwards.

02 Physics
- Rope and plank bridges are built from jointed rigid bodies. Planks crack and drop out from under you after a short delay, so you can't stop on them.
- Crumbling floors, rolling-boulder traps, pressure plates, swinging blades, dart traps, and pushable stone blocks for puzzles.
- Loot has real weight. Carrying more slows you down and drains stamina faster.

03 Sun, time and lighting
- A real sun moves across the sky in a compressed 12 to 15 minute day, casting shadows.
- Sunlight only reaches Zones A and B, through openings, cracks and the missing roof, so the sun's angle changes which areas are lit.
- Color grading shifts from dawn to noon to a red-orange sunset to night.

04 Ticking clock
- An always-visible countdown to darkness, drawn as a stone sundial on the HUD. It ticks audibly, and the ticking speeds up and gets louder in the last 2 minutes.
- At 60 seconds left, the screen edges pulse, the music shifts, and the temple rumbles.
- At zero, darkness falls, the guardians wake, and the great door begins closing, leaving a short final window to escape.
- In Zone C you can't see the sky, so the sundial on the HUD and the sounds are your only sense of time.

05 Torch: the only light underground
- Fully dungeoned scenes have zero ambient light: no glowing decorations, no fill light, no skybox bleed. Outside your torch radius it's true black.
- The torch is a dynamic point light with flicker and real shadows.
- Fuel: the torch burns fuel, its radius shrinks as fuel runs low, and water, wind gusts or a fall can blow it out. Relighting takes a short, vulnerable animation.
- Spare light: you can find limited fuel and spare torches. You can also place torches in wall sconces to light your way back out.
- Guardians: they're drawn to torchlight, so light keeps you alive and also gives you away.

06 Relics and economy
- At least 15 relic types (idols, masks, gem-studded urns, scrolls and so on) with different values, weights and rarities, each worth more the deeper you find it.
- Some relics are trapped: lifting one off its pedestal triggers a trap unless you swap in an object of equal weight.
- Show a running loot value on the HUD. Only loot carried out the door counts.

07 Guardians at sunset
- At least 3 guardian types, each with different movement and senses (for example a slow stone sentinel, a fast crawler that hunts by sound, and a shadow that avoids light).
- They spawn deepest first and sweep outward toward the door.
- They chase you with real navmesh pathfinding, not straight-line homing.
- Getting caught means you lose the run and everything you were carrying.

08 Player
- Pointer-lock first-person controls: run, jump, crouch, climb vines and ledges, and stamina.
- Grab, carry, drop and throw objects.

09 Meta progression and UI
- A results screen showing haul value, time and relics found.
- A persistent high-score table, plus a shop that sells gear upgrades between runs (better torch, more fuel, rope, a bigger satchel). Save with localStorage.
- Main menu, pause menu, settings (FOV, sensitivity, volume, graphics quality), a HUD (sundial clock, stamina, weight, torch fuel, loot value), and a debug overlay (FPS, physics body count, current scene).

10 Audio
All procedural: jungle ambience by day, deep rumbles at dusk, the clock ticking, creaking and snapping wood, stone grinding, the torch crackling, guardian calls, and a heartbeat when guardians are near.

Stretch goals
A hand-drawn map that fills in as you explore, a collapse sequence triggered by taking the main idol, a ghost replay of your best run, and gamepad support.

Process
- Before writing any code, write two files:
  - DESIGN.md: the core loop, the scene graph with its unlock dependencies, a relic table, and loot value by zone.
  - ARCHITECTURE.md: how the code is structured.
- Write unit tests for the loot economy, trap triggers, countdown thresholds, the torch fuel and radius curve, scene unlock logic, and the guardian state machines.
- Finish with an honest SELF_REVIEW.md. Mark every requirement as done, partial or missing, list known bugs and FPS per zone, and confirm that Zone C has zero ambient light. Anything claimed but broken is scored harder than anything admitted as missing.

Work autonomously until a full run is playable and fun, from arriving at dawn to escaping or dying at dark. Don't stop at a prototype.
